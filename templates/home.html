Artificial Intelligence-Based Early Detection of Dengue using CBC Data

Project Abstract

Dengue fever remains a critical public health concern in tropical and subtropical regions, necessitating rapid and accurate early detection mechanisms. Complete Blood Count (CBC) parameters have demonstrated significant diagnostic relevance in identifying dengue infections. Existing approaches primarily rely on traditional machine learning and ensemble-based techniques for classification, achieving strong predictive performance. However, these methods often do not explicitly model the complex interdependencies among hematological biomarkers.

This study proposes a deep learning–based sequential modeling approach utilizing a Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) network for dengue prediction using CBC parameters. The proposed architecture captures nonlinear inter-feature relationships by treating hematological attributes as ordered sequences, enabling enhanced representation learning. The model incorporates feature scaling, regularization techniques, and optimized hyperparameters to improve generalization performance. Experimental evaluation demonstrates superior classification capability, achieving high predictive accuracy along with strong performance in precision, recall, F1-score, and ROC-AUC metrics. Additionally, the system is implemented with a deployment-ready prediction module capable of providing probability estimates, confidence levels, and decision-making time.

The results indicate that recurrent deep learning architectures can effectively model complex biomarker interactions for dengue prediction, offering a robust alternative to conventional ensemble-based methods. This work highlights the potential of sequential deep neural networks in advancing intelligent clinical decision-support systems for early dengue detection.
